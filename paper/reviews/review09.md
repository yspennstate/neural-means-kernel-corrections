# Reviewer 09 — statistical inference and scope

**Vote on the current submission: NO (revise, with a credible path to a publishable empirical-methods paper).**

I reviewed the attached main-paper text and the released source in `source_repo/paper/`, plus statistical scripts and stored numerical records. My vote is about the current claims and their consistency, not an allegation that the underlying experiments are invalid. The strongest reported comparisons survive the checks below; the required repairs are principally inferential and expository.

## Findings requiring revision

1. **Numerical proximity to PARA-Net has not established statistical equivalence.** The abstract and Sections 1 and 4.4 describe 4.546% versus a published 4.55% as a “tie,” justified by comparing the difference with an assumed test-block uncertainty. Section 4.4 commendably discloses that the comparator variance and covariance are unknown and calls the 0.020-percentage-point calculation a sensitivity calculation. That disclosure does not justify the subsequent claim that the difference is “therefore not a separation at all.” Failure to establish superiority is not evidence of equivalence. The comparator is also rounded to two decimals, so its rounding interval alone contains the new mean. Use: “numerically close to the published 4.55%; neither superiority nor equivalence can be assessed without the comparator's per-case predictions and retraining information.” Describe lower published numbers elsewhere as numerical benchmark orderings, not significance statements. If equivalence is essential, specify a scientific equivalence margin and obtain a confidence interval for an appropriate contrast; rewriting the claim is sufficient for this revision.

2. **The supplement contains stronger, unsupported uncertainty claims than the revised main paper.** `paper/supp_experiments.tex`, subsection “Which uncertainty is the binding one,” still says the 0.098-point PCA-Net separation is “five of those and real,” and that both imputed uncertainty figures are “conservative” because errors on identical test points are positively correlated. Neither comparator variance nor positive covariance follows from identical test inputs. With per-case errors A and B the paired variance is `(Var(A)+Var(B)-2Cov(A,B))/n`. The released data provide neither `Var(B)` nor `Cov(A,B)` for these historical methods. Remove the inferential claim and the conservativeness assertion throughout the supplement, and align it with the narrower revised main-paper discussion. Calling missing paired data an “unpaired comparison” does not turn the observations into independent test samples.

3. **The empirical convex-pool certificate must be distinguished from a population or future-seed floor everywhere.** The computed 4.814% bound is a valid algebraic lower bound on RMS relative error for convex combinations of the specific sixty prediction arrays on their particular evaluation set. I independently reconstructed the calculation from `campaign/collected/dgx/seedarch.json`: the smallest diagonal gives `e_min=4.9509666%`, normalized within-group minimum is `0.98079628`, between-group minimum `0.93779682`, and the displayed block bound is `4.8136114%`. The stored hindsight convex optimum is `4.8761329%`, so the reported gap is approximately 0.06252 percentage points. These numbers support the finite-pool empirical claim. They do not estimate a lower confidence bound on population risk, and the minima among ten seeds cannot bind an eleventh seed. In particular, the supplement's statement that “no number of its own seeds can pass” the measured floor and that “the seed axis is exhausted at ten” is unsupported. Replace it with “the ten-seed averages are close to the empirical lower bounds for convex mixtures of the ten trained instances.” Theorem 6.6's infinite-pool consequences require its pairwise inequalities to hold uniformly over that larger pool; say so explicitly. A fresh architecture or seed may violate those hypotheses. The paper already gives useful limitations about solver error; maintain them and avoid treating a finite-pool result as a data-generating error floor.

4. **Seed counts and pair counts are descriptive replication evidence, not independent population experiments.** Seeds redraw validation splits and initialization but reuse the same training pool and fixed test block. Conditional-on-dataset seed variability is meaningful and the main paper says this. The words “independent replicates” should always identify that conditioning. Likewise, 840 OCO pair assessments reuse networks and the same test cases. Section 5.2 appropriately acknowledges that dependence; carry that qualification into summaries of success counts. Do not derive binomial significance from those counts, and do not let mean ± seed SD imply a confidence interval for population performance. A two-way case/seed analysis would be needed for a new aggregate population inference; it is unnecessary if the conclusions are kept conditional and descriptive.

5. **Conformal validity is mathematically well motivated but its empirical check needs narrower language.** The scripts compute the correct order statistic for the displayed levels: with 1,000 calibration cases, the 90% index is 901 and the 95% index is 951. `uq_conformal_plam.py` partitions the fixed 20,000-case block using a seeded random permutation and uses the held-out predictions without refitting on that calibration subset. Under independent i.i.d. cases, a fixed fitted pipeline, and continuous scores, the Beta / Beta-binomial comparison is correct. Conditional on one realized calibration sample the evaluation count is binomial; after integrating calibration randomness it is Beta-binomial. Make that conditioning explicit in the supplement's wording. Observing all ten coverages inside individual 95% central bands is a compatibility check, not proof that independence or exchangeability holds, and the checks across seeds are dependent because their data overlap. “Fixed splits provide exchangeability” should be replaced by “the assumed i.i.d. sampling and the split's independence from the frozen fitting procedure provide exchangeability.” Any test-informed researcher selection is outside the exact guarantee; do not claim it is ruled out merely by inspection of the splitting function.

## Positive checks and reasons the work can survive revision

- The paper distinguishes mean relative error from RMS relative error; the floor comparison is conducted in the RMS metric. This is essential and correct.
- The structural-mechanics seed pipeline uses the same bootstrap row indices for both members of a within-seed contrast. `campaign/seed_pipeline.py` therefore implements a paired case bootstrap rather than subtracting unrelated intervals.
- `campaign/jpl_seeded.py` likewise reuses the same 2,000 bootstrap row-index draws for combination-minus-emulator contrasts and stores intervals for both reduced and reconstructed-radiance metrics. This is the right structure for conditional-on-fitted-model case uncertainty.
- The OCO text discloses the weak-CO2 radiance exception, the different budgets, and the distinction between rescoring reduced-representation predictions and demonstrating full retrieval performance. These limitations improve the paper and should be retained.
- The 4.814% finite-pool certificate is reproducible from the stored second-moment matrix without rerunning training. It is useful as a restricted empirical diagnostic, even though it is not a lower bound on the attainable error of the learning problem.

## Minimal sufficient revision

Remove “tie” and inferential comparisons with unobserved historical baselines; synchronize the supplement with those changes; explicitly place hats on empirical second-moment quantities or state once that empirical calculations use the empirical test measure; qualify all infinite-seed extrapolations; and distinguish conditional, marginal, and across-seed interpretations of the uncertainty results. No claim of mathematical equivalence, population error floor, or independent 840-trial validation should remain. With those repairs, my statistical assessment becomes **YES for submission as a carefully delimited empirical study**, subject to the other reviewers' mathematical, novelty, and reproducibility findings. Journal acceptance cannot be assured by this vote.
