\subsection{Coverage under split-conformal assumptions}

The native-space bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:or} contains an unknown norm,
and $P_\lambda$ ranks the measured pointwise errors weakly. A separate
calibration construction rescales this positive score by an empirical
quantile. Write $\widehat G$ for the fitted predictor,
$e(u)=G(u)-\widehat G(u)$, and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf}
s_i=\frac{\|e(\tilde u_i)\|_2}{P_\lambda(\tilde u_i)},\qquad
k=\lceil(1-\alpha)(m+1)\rceil,\qquad
Q=\begin{cases}s_{(k)},&k\le m,\\+\infty,&k=m+1,\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $s_{(k)}$ is the $k$-th order statistic of the $m$ calibration scores
and $0<\alpha<1$. When the required index exceeds the calibration sample
size the prediction set is the whole output space. A fixed random split
alone does not establish the independence from model development required
below.

\begin{proposition}[finite-sample coverage]\label{prop:conf}
Fix the predictor $\widehat G$, design $X$ and positive scale function
$P_\lambda$. Suppose the calibration and test inputs
$\tilde u_1,\ldots,\tilde u_m,u^\star$ are exchangeable and independent of
these fitted objects. Define $Q$ by \eqref{eq:conf} and
\[
C(u)=\{v:\|v-\widehat G(u)\|_2\le QP_\lambda(u)\}.
\]
Then $\mathbb P(G(u^\star)\in C(u^\star))\ge1-\alpha$. If the scores
are almost surely distinct, their coverage is exactly $k/(m+1)$, and hence
is at most $1-\alpha+1/(m+1)$. When $k=m+1$, the coverage is one,
regardless of ties.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Supplement~\ref{proof:prop:conf}.


\begin{remark}[conditional reference law for realized coverage]\label{rem:covlaw}
Suppose, more strongly, that conditional on the fixed fitted predictor and
scale function, calibration and future evaluation scores are i.i.d. from a
continuous distribution $F$. If $1\le k\le m$, the coverage probability
conditional on the calibration sample is $F(s_{(k)})$. The probability
integral transform makes $F(s_i)$ i.i.d. uniform, so
\[
F(s_{(k)})\sim\mathrm{Beta}(k,m+1-k).
\]
This is the standard order-statistic law \citep{vovk2012conditional}.
Conditional on the calibration sample, the count of covered cases in an
independent evaluation set of size $N$ is binomial with success probability
$F(s_{(k)})$; integrating gives a Beta--binomial count. For $k=m+1$ the
conditional coverage is instead identically one and the count is $N$;
there is no Beta distribution with a zero second parameter. Exchangeability
alone is sufficient for Proposition~\ref{prop:conf}, but does not imply this
stronger Beta--binomial law.
\end{remark}

The proposition is the usual split-conformal rank argument
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution}; its proof is in
Supplement~\ref{proof:prop:conf}. The score is a residual norm divided by a
positive scale, and the resulting set is a Euclidean ball in output space
with radius $QP_\lambda(u)$. The stored statistic named \texttt{mean\_width}
in the campaign records is the mean of this radius, not its diameter.
The guarantee is marginal over a new input, not conditional coverage at each
input and not a collection of coordinatewise intervals. Constant-radius and
other positive-scale scores satisfy the same proposition under the same
independence assumptions, whether or not the scale ranks error well.

The released campaign applies this calculation retrospectively to a
$1000/19000$ partition of the public test block after the reported models
were fitted. Its recorded six-member coverage is
$\uqPlamSix\pm\uqPlamSixSd\%$ at the nominal $\uqCoverNom\%$ level,
with $P_\lambda$ rebuilt from each seed's correction kernel
(\texttt{campaign/uq\_conformal\_plam.py}). Under the i.i.d.-continuous
reference model, $m=1000$, $\alpha=0.1$ give
$\mathrm{Beta}(901,100)$ conditional coverage, and the central $95\%$
reference interval for the fraction on $N=19000$ cases is
$[88.0\%,91.8\%]$; at the $95\%$ nominal level it is
$[93.5\%,96.3\%]$. The reported ten seeds fall inside these intervals
at both levels. These are conditional reference comparisons, not a
verification of independence or a theorem about the retrospective exercise.
The seeds share test cases and are not ten independent draws from this law.

A public test block can influence model development even when its rows do
not enter an optimizer. The retrospective partition therefore does not by
itself certify the fresh-calibration conditions of the proposition; prospective
coverage would require a predictor and scale fixed before an independent,
exchangeable calibration-and-evaluation sample is observed. The measured
coverage, the constant-radius comparison and the radius ratios remain empirical
results. Under the stated fresh-sample assumptions, the conformal guarantee
does not require native-space membership, a known residual norm, or a
positive association between $P_\lambda$ and error.
