# Independent reviewer 15

**Vote: NO — do not publish the submitted version unchanged.**

**Scope:** I read the complete supplied manuscript text, checked the experiment and implementation portions of the repository supplement, inspected the OCO-2 loader and table generator, the prediction and stacking paths, and executed the repository's reported-macro audit. I did not read other reviewers' reports. This is an audit of reporting and reproducibility, not an independent rerun of the expensive experiments.

## What is supported

The released summary records are unusually substantial. Running `python campaign/audit_reported_macros.py` from `source_repo` completed successfully and reported **0 macro disagreements**. In particular, the stored aggregates support 4.546% for the six-member mechanics pipeline, 5.433% ± 0.093% for the low-data pipeline, and the OCO-2 combination numbers. The manuscript usefully distinguishes seed variability from test-case variability, acknowledges the missing paired published predictions, limits OCO-2 conclusions to reduced-representation rescoring, reports the WCO2 radiance exception, and admits that validation reuse invalidates a direct application of its candidate-class generalization bounds. The negative uncertainty-ranking and readout-control results add value. I found no basis here to accuse the underlying results of fabrication.

## Required reporting repairs

1. **OCO-2 metric attribution contradicts the paper's own careful definition.** Table 1 says the comparison uses “both of that paper’s error metrics.” Section 5 instead says both criteria are defined here on the released reduced representation, and neither is the source study's full-spectrum acceptance criterion. The loader `jpl_data.py` computes relative coefficient error and retained-PCA reconstructed-radiance error, consistent with Section 5. Repair the caption to say “the two rescoring criteria defined in Section 5.” Retain the existing exclusions of PCA truncation, instrument response/noise, Jacobians and retrieval-level accuracy.

2. **The claimed simultaneous dominance of every individual model is false in the displayed table.** The last paragraph of Section 6.2 says the validation-selected combination improves on every single model on both metrics. Table 6 gives reduced error 4.12% for the combination and 4.11% for the flat-feature Matérn head. The macro audit reproduces that ordering. Replace this with the accurate statement: the combination improves both rescoring criteria relative to the released reference emulator on average, while trading a small amount of reduced accuracy for better radiance accuracy relative to the best reduced-metric head. Keep the per-seed WCO2 exception.

3. **The cross-fitting description overstates the actual implementation.** The paragraph after Theorem 6.9 says each training row's members are out of fold. In `campaign/stack_correct_seeded5.py`, `member_files` uses `krr_oof_train.npy` for the kernel member, but uses each neural member's `_predtr` array. `gen_preds.py` loads one saved trained neural model and evaluates it on training inputs; it does not train a fold-specific neural model. The refiner's kernel input is OOF, which does not make the refiner itself OOF. Describe the actual mixed construction. For a theorem related to deployment, introduce the rowwise discrepancy between the training pseudo-mean and deployed full mean and give the additional term `||k(u,X)(K+n lambda I)^(-1) Delta_X||`; do not call all neural predictions cross-fitted. This is a mismatch in the theorem-to-code narrative, not proof that test targets leaked into training.

4. **Some remaining prose overstates the finite-pool evidence.** Section 4.5 says the residual “is not a function of the load in any way a Matérn RKHS on R41 can see.” A finite kernel grid at one sample-size regime cannot establish that. The deterministic-solver target also makes irreducible noise a particularly strong inference. Replace this with a statement that the tested Matérn corrections recovered little additional signal. Section 7 calls these the “last few learnable hundredths” and says they are “not a step on the way to zero”; neither follows from the tested pool. Say the improvements were small over the capacities, architectures, schedules and training sizes tested. The manuscript already explains common approximation bias and the missing refined-solver experiment; make the affirmative prose obey those caveats too.

5. **Make the empirical floor's scope explicit wherever its numeric value is interpreted.** Theorem 6.6 is a population second-moment statement under its assumptions. Plugging in the observed second-moment matrix supplies an exact algebraic lower bound for convex mixtures on that finite evaluation block. It does not furnish a confidence lower bound for population risk or a bound over untrained future architectures. The 4.814% / 4.876% comparison is a legitimate finite-pool, finite-block diagnostic. Say that directly in the abstract and the table/paragraph presenting the numbers. Do not imply that the theorem explains the extra gain from the nonconvex per-pixel-plus-kernel pipeline or controls generalization of hindsight weights.

6. **Preserve the real reproducibility limitation.** README and Supplement S9 correctly disclose that the 3.84 GB residual archive and approximately 250 MB OCO-2 prediction archive are retained on the campaign host rather than distributed with the repository. This means table aggregation can be reproduced immediately, while array-level correlations, pairing, selection diagnostics and conformal checks cannot be independently reconstructed from the released summaries alone without those archives or retraining. The main availability statement should give this distinction as plainly as the supplement. Deposit the archives with a persistent identifier if available; otherwise do not claim public release of the arrays or manufacture one. The present repository checksums/manifests are useful, but are not substitutes for access to the arrays.

7. **Proof discoverability needs the user-requested repair.** The first-page GitHub footnote should explicitly point readers to `paper/supplement.pdf` for the supplement and proofs. Place an individual proof pointer after each theorem/proposition/corollary whose proof is external, including the many statements in Section 6. A generic sentence that all proofs are in S8 is helpful but does not satisfy the requested local discoverability.

## Secondary issues

“Tie” against PARA-Net should consistently mean numerical proximity on the shared benchmark, not demonstrated statistical equivalence: the source predictions and training variability are unavailable, and the paper's imputed variance calculation is only a sensitivity exercise. Likewise, the statement that everything is replicated at ten seeds needs its local exceptions retained: the feature/norm diagnostic is one seed, some kernel-flow checks use five, the extended budget result is one seed, and the suite survey is uncontrolled.

## Publishability after repair

The underlying work is plausibly publishable as a carefully delimited empirical operator-learning study with a supporting algebraic/theoretical toolkit. The existing public records give stronger support than a single-seed table. My NO is driven by identifiable internal contradictions and overextended interpretations, not by a requirement to discover a new method. The narrative repairs above can make a substantially more credible submission without inventing results or rerunning all experiments. Editorial acceptance remains journal-dependent, and complete public array-level reproducibility remains conditional on the retained archives becoming accessible.
