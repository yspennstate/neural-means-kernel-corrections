# Independent review 03

**Vote on publishing the supplied version: NO.** This is a repairable empirical paper, not a failed research project. I would support submission after a focused correction of the claims and a reproducibility release that matches the stated scope. My objection is to publishing the present wording as a finished, fully supported account, not to publishing this work in any venue.

I read the supplied 39-page manuscript in extracted form and checked the closest prior work and the public repository through primary sources. I did not read other reviews or edit the manuscript. I have not independently rerun the experiments or inspected all retained prediction arrays. The source repository is publicly accessible; a direct web fetch initially failed, but opening its successfully indexed result worked. That initial failure is not evidence of a missing repository.

## Contribution and novelty

The defensible contribution is a replicated diagnostic study of known neural/kernel constructions: what residual correction, learned representations, architectural diversity, and coordinate selection contribute on two fixed benchmark protocols. Ten-seed comparisons, frozen-feature linear-readout controls, explicit RMS/mean distinctions, and the failed UQ rankings are useful evidence. The study could fit a computational science, applied machine learning, or reproducibility-oriented venue. Acceptance by any particular journal remains a judgment for its editors and reviewers.

The paper correctly acknowledges that neither neural-mean GP regression nor learned-feature kernels are new. Mora et al. explicitly parameterize a GP mean by a neural operator and train by likelihood; their published bibliographic metadata agree with the paper. [Mora et al., primary author repository](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/tx6zm-xsq12)

Wilson et al. explicitly learn neural input transformations inside GP kernels, jointly through marginal likelihood; the paper's description and PMLR citation are accurate. [Wilson et al., AISTATS 2016](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v51/wilson16.html)

REEF-GP is real, was submitted in June 2026, and fits a residual GP to a frozen neural operator using internal embeddings. Thus post-hoc fitting on learned features cannot be claimed as a new construction here. The current related-work paragraph recognizes this. Preserve that acknowledgment and present the controlled predictor comparison as the contribution. [Vendrell-Gallart et al., primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17513v1)

The second-moment identity and equicorrelation calculation belong to classical ensemble theory, as the manuscript says. The block refinement for seeds and architectures is useful bookkeeping for the experiment, but is elementary and should not carry the burden of a major theoretical novelty claim. The cited ambiguity-decomposition antecedent exists and has the stated authors. The conference was NIPS 1994 and the volume appeared in 1995, so the manuscript's 1995 date is defensible. [Krogh and Vedelsby, proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/1994/hash/b8c37e33defde51cf91e1e03e51657da-Abstract.html)

## Required repairs

1. **Make the OCO-2 claim uniform throughout.** Table 1's caption says the rows use both error metrics of Lamminpää et al. Sections 5 and 5.2 correctly explain that these are two newly defined rescoring criteria on the released reduced representation, excluding full-spectrum truncation and instrument/noise effects. The introduction also slips into saying both metrics of that problem. Replace all such language with “two retained-PCA rescoring criteria defined here.” The source paper evaluates reconstructed spectra and instrument-noise-scaled errors, and also studies Jacobians and retrievals; those are not established by the present comparison. Its published metadata are accurate. [Lamminpää et al., AMT 2025](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/18/673/2025/)

2. **Remove claims that the pullback identity predicts a reduction or proves the measured mechanism.** Proposition 6.8 identifies the pullback RKHS and gives a conditional bound if the target factors through a feature map with an RKHS readout. It gives no comparison with the raw-input RKHS norm. Ratios of fitted norms are ratios of lower bounds, as the paper elsewhere acknowledges; they do not prove a ratio of true target norms. “No rescaling can imitate that” is also too general: linear feature maps can be rescalings, and an adapted metric can exactly represent some maps. Say the tested rescalings recovered much less of this particular empirical gap. In the discussion, replace the statement that the fitted norm gives the quantitative reason to spend capacity on residuals with a statement that it is an observed finite-design diagnostic consistent with the conditional bound.

3. **Keep the residual-floor claim about the trained pool.** The paper usefully disclaims a demonstrated data floor, but repeatedly reintroduces it through statements that these are the last learnable hundredths, that they are not steps toward zero, or that modeling limitations should yield to the tested changes. A deterministic solver's discretization error relative to continuum truth is still part of the deterministic target being learned; it does not by itself create irreducible prediction noise. The measured theorem limits convex combinations of this pool on this distribution/design, not other predictors, retraining schemes, or the continuum model. Retain the correlations, localization, and limited scaling evidence; state the solver explanation as an untested hypothesis.

4. **Repair overstated consequences of conditional theory.** Section 6.1 honestly says the validation capacity hypotheses fail for the shipped pipeline. Section 6.2 nevertheless opens by saying Proposition S1.3 makes fitting the weights safe, and Section 3 calls it statistically almost free. Those unconditional assurances should be removed. Similarly, Theorem 6.9's caveat says the cross-fitted correction and deployed full-data mean do not match. Either show the discrepancy term explicitly or avoid statements that the reported pipeline carries the unmodified kernel bound. The algebraic bound is useful without presenting it as a verified deployed error certificate.

5. **State the hypotheses for the exact conformal distribution.** Exchangeability alone gives the usual lower marginal coverage guarantee. The upper bound stated in Section 6.5 fails with ties; for example all scores can equal zero and coverage is one. The Beta law additionally needs iid calibration/evaluation scores with continuous conditional score distribution (or an appropriate randomized construction), conditional on the independently fitted model. State those hypotheses, use the lower bound without them, and describe the Beta-binomial bands as applying under those stronger conditions. A fixed-model argument should not be described as conditional on an already realized calibration sample, because the coverage is then a fixed number, not a Beta random variable.

6. **Match the data-availability statement to the actual release.** The public README lists code, a lockfile, data checksums, and per-run records. It explicitly says the approximately 3.84-GB structural residual archive and 250-MB OCO member predictions are retained on the campaign host, available on request, and intended for later deposition. Therefore there is a real public release, but its current contents do not allow all array-level checks from the repository alone. State this distinction in the manuscript; pin the repository commit and ideally deposit the missing arrays or a documented regeneration pipeline before claiming full independent reproduction. The parent located source commit `50c05ff1cce32d20df446c5c57c1c20027df5875` and reports that its main PDF matches the attachment. I have not independently verified that match. [Public repository and availability statement](https://github.com/yspennstate/neural-means-kernel-corrections)

## Citation details and smaller corrections

- **Do not remove David Pitt from Ma et al.** The older arXiv record has three authors, but the final TMLR publication has Ziqi Ma, David Pitt, Kamyar Azizzadenesheli, and Anima Anandkumar. The manuscript's author list is correct for the final article. Cite the final publication URL directly to avoid combining final metadata with the older preprint's metadata. [NVIDIA author publication page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/lpr/publication/ma2024calibrated/)
- Kumar et al.'s cited work exists with the listed authors. The bibliography should at least add its missing identifier `arXiv:2404.15618`, or use its final publication metadata after checking them. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15618)
- The Calandra et al. authors, venue, and pages match the primary institutional record. [UCL accepted manuscript record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10083570/)
- Section 3 says all networks use the reported metric and that this beats normalized MSE, whereas Table 4 makes normalized MSE the best MLP variant. Name the actual variants and their losses rather than making the universal claim.
- Corollary 6.4 bounds the optimum and supplies an upper bound from uniform weights. The prose “whatever convex ensemble one builds” is too strong: an arbitrary convex combination can be much worse than the optimum.
- Proposition 6.7's strict derivative positivity needs a nondegenerate correlation assumption; at perfect anticorrelation, its displayed derivative with respect to the error ratio is zero. Add `-1 < rho < min(t,1/t)` if retaining strict inequalities.
- Table 6 says the wider tuning grid is being run. A final paper should either include the actual resulting experiment or remove the ongoing-work sentence and state simply that the reported grid has a boundary optimum. Do not invent its outcome.

## Minimum publishable revision

Keep the core experiment and its numbers, provided they are reproduced from the released records. Recast the paper as a careful empirical diagnosis supported by classical conditional identities and bounds. Repair the six issues above, provide the supplement in the submission, make code/data availability exact, and shorten the repeated causal language. No new theorem, fabricated experiment, or claim of solving the benchmark floor is needed. A deeper scientific claim that the benchmark itself has an irreducible error floor would require the independent refined-solver experiment already identified by the author; editing cannot supply that evidence.

**Verdict after those minimum repairs: potentially publishable and worth submitting; present-version vote remains NO.**
