\begin{abstract}
A kernel fitted after a neural network can correct its residuals or replace its output layer. We study these two roles on public operator-learning problems, repeating the main experiments at ten seeds. On structural mechanics, a residual kernel correction of a six-member stack reaches $\errSix\pm\errSixSd\%$ mean relative test error, close to the published PARA-Net score of $\pubBestHi\%$, without establishing statistical equivalence. In the 1250-label regime, the pipeline reaches $\errBestLow\pm\errBestLowSd\%$ against a published $\pubBestLo\%$. On OCO-2, the same Mat\'ern regression scores approximately $40\%$ reduced error on raw inputs and $4.1\%$ on frozen neural features in the O2 band. A per-coordinate combination improves on the source emulator's stored predictions in the ten-seed means under both retained-representation rescoring criteria on all three bands; full-spectrum and retrieval accuracy are outside this comparison. Residual alignment explains the limited gains from averaging the trained mechanics predictors: a block lower bound of $\saBound\%$ lies close to the hindsight-optimal global convex RMS error of $\saSixtyOracleRms\%$ for the sixty-member pool. Supporting ensemble identities and conditional native-space bounds clarify the scope of these measurements. Calibrated uncertainty sets are also compared; a constant-width set is narrower on average than one scaled by the kernel power function. The public test blocks were examined across several campaigns, so the results constitute retrospective benchmark evidence.
\end{abstract}

The base source for this revision was commit \texttt{50c05ff1cce3} (full identifier in the source-package README), whose main PDF matches the original supplied manuscript. The repository's \texttt{paper/} directory contains the current article, supplement, and editable sources. Full checkpoints and large prediction/residual arrays are not bundled in the public repository; the source record reports them as retained and available on request. The
structural-mechanics, Helmholtz, Navier--Stokes and advection data are
distributed through the data record accompanying \citet{dehoop2022cost}
(\texttt{data.caltech.edu}, record \texttt{20091}); the OCO-2 emulation data
and the kernel-flow emulator's predictions through the OSF project
\texttt{u2t8a}; and the ClimSim data through the LEAP repository
\texttt{subsampled\_low\_res}.
